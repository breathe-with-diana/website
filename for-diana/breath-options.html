The one breath, seven ways

Tapping Take one breath with me on your page opens a full screen moment: everything else fades back, and one shape breathes with you for eleven seconds while the words change from breathe in to and let it go. Right now that shape is a circle getting bigger and smaller, which does the job but says nothing.

Here are six alternatives, plus the current one to compare against. Every one of them keeps the same physiology as the page: eleven seconds, four seconds filling, seven seconds emptying, and no easing that a body would not recognise. Each preview below loops so you can compare them at a glance; See it full screen plays a single breath exactly as a visitor would meet it.

Option 4, the seed of life, is the one we picked, and it is live on your page now in both English and Spanish. The rest stay here so the choice can be revisited. Tap the heart on any of them, and leave a note if something should change.

Option 1 The dot, the original

One point of terracotta swelling and settling inside a soft halo. It is honest and it matches the breathing full stop in the headline, but it is also the most generic thing on a page that is otherwise full of character. Kept here for comparison.

Option 2 The dandelion opening

Your own logo, breathing. The filaments fan open on the in-breath, and on the long out-breath four seeds let go and drift away. Nothing else here says this is Diana's page so immediately, and the metaphor is the work itself: you fill, then you release, and something leaves you.

Option 3 The flower opening

Two rings of petals opening from a centre and closing again. The most literal reading of “a breath” and the easiest to understand in one second. The risk is that an opening lotus is the single most used image in the wellness world, so it costs you some of the distinctness the rest of the page has earned.

Option 4 The seed of life chosen · live now

Six circles drawing themselves out of one on the in-breath, folding back into it on the out-breath. Quiet, precise, and it rewards a second look because the figure only completes at the top of the inhale. It leans spiritual, which suits the deep door and may sit oddly beside the gentle one.

Option 5 A pair of lungs

Two soft lobes filling and emptying below a slim airway. The most immediately readable of all seven, and the only one that names the body directly. Kept deliberately abstract, as leaf shapes rather than anatomy, because anything more accurate turns the moment clinical.

Option 6 Rings on water

Three hairline rings spreading outward from a still point, each a beat behind the one before. Closest in spirit to what is there now, just with something to watch. It is the calmest of the six and the one least likely to ever irritate on a second visit.

Option 7 Your own breath wave

The wave already drawn in your practice chapter, with the gold point travelling it: up the rise for the four seconds in, down the long fall for the seven seconds out. It makes the ritual and the explanation the same picture, and it is the only option that shows the shape of the breath rather than just its size.

In calm mode, or for anyone whose device asks for reduced motion, the circles do not travel at all: the figure stays complete and only the ink strength breathes.
